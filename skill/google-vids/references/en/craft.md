# Direction and editing

These are creative decision aids, not Google requirements. Choose them for the
brief: there is no universal scene count, typeface, tempo or template.

## Direction and representative scene

Record a few decisions in the existing working notes: the intended impression
and its purpose, what stays recognizable, what changes across scenes, movement
character/information density, and two concrete approaches that would miss this
brief. “Modern” is not enough to choose images.

Find the scene where the value becomes visible. Test its entrance, action and
result. For a dynamic short video, first test the opening through the first
completed event: an attractive climax cannot validate an empty opening. A blind
reviewer should describe what actually changed and why they would keep watching.
A deliberately quiet opening may be appropriate to another brief. Do not repeat
this entire process for a small caption correction.

Compare neighboring frames for scale, composition and action. The same object
can provide causal continuity; changing it only for variety can damage the story.
Vary presentation when it otherwise repeats without development. If changing the
title would make the video fit any unrelated service, find more specific action.

## Meaning and structure

Choose a visual idea that connects opening and result: a continuous action,
metaphor, demonstration or emotional change. Know what the viewer should
understand, feel and remember. Make the opening offer a reason to watch: a result,
question, recognizable situation or strong image. Then develop it with evidence,
action, explanation or change. End the thought. A CTA belongs where the purpose
requires a viewer action, not automatically in every editorial piece.

Every scene has work to do. Combine repeated points or change the way the next
scene advances them. Transitions preserve causal connections, not merely color.
A talking character is one presentation option; use demonstrations, details,
wide shots and meaningful cutaways to show what the speech is about.

For a compact scene map, record purpose, spoken line, visible action/result,
source/reference, shot scale, important timing, audio event and acceptance
criterion. Keep it proportional to the project rather than creating paperwork.

## Service introduction and promotion

Identify the actual audience problem, concrete offer, visible proof and next
step. A list of fashionable technologies does not show a useful service. Prefer
a small before → action → consequence demonstration. Do not invent product
screens, metrics, testimonials or performance claims. A mockup can illustrate a
workflow if it is clearly distinguished from a working product.

Use a real provided contact/channel when the brief calls for one. Do not invent
contact details or stall an otherwise complete nonpromotional story for a CTA.
Give the closing information enough stable time to read; louder animation cannot
compensate for an unclear offer.

## Frame and readability

Choose the intended aspect ratio early. Establish a hierarchy: one primary idea,
a supporting line when needed, and subordinate context. Avoid duplicating the
whole voiceover on screen unless the viewing mode requires captions. Keep text
inside verified safe areas for the actual destination; do not turn one platform's
example margin into a universal rule.

Design the fully visible frame before animating it. Check contrast, actual text
length, line breaks, Cyrillic/other required glyphs and native font fallback.
A preset's Latin preview does not establish the rendered language. Use distinct
foreground/background roles and enough visual rest. Exported PNG text is not
separately editable in Vids: use native text where future editing matters.

## Continuity and movement

An event changes the subject or its relationship to the world. A camera orbit
around a static prop does not substitute for the requested prop action. Define
initial state → action → result, and check those states in the pixels. Keep
identity, shape, contact, lighting and essential background stable unless change
is intended. Consistent color alone cannot repair a broken spatial connection.

Animate to direct attention or explain a relationship. Separate entrance, hold
and exit. A graphic needed through the cut must not disappear because an exit
animation was inherited. Avoid resetting the same photo effect across adjacent
shots, and inspect the final frames for looping or scale jumps. Transitions and
camera motion are tools, not evidence of a lively scene.

## Rhythm and sound

Time scenes to the actual action, sentence, reading load and emotional turn.
Alternate density and rest where useful. Cut on meaning, reaction or a completed
action; do not leave an empty tail or enforce equal scene lengths. One continuous
sentence can support several shots. A deliberate hold is not automatically a
fault because a freeze detector reports little pixel change.

Stage the action answered by a line within the shot: time its generated beat to
the word and keep the character's reaction in the same take; see
[person and object in one take](generation.md#person-and-object-in-one-take).
With burned captions, consider a cut at the start of a word, preferably a phrase;
verify the actual caption behavior using [caption checks](russian-and-speech.md#captions-and-time-domains).
A detail cutaway can cover an inter-phrase pause. Splitting one continuous take
into adjacent scenes with contiguous source ranges can preserve an invisible
picture cut while aligning one action with two spoken accents.

Treat voice, music and event sound as separate layers. Music can carry a phrase
or transition; a small motivated SFX can make an event tangible. Do not add sound
to every movement. Check whether scene changes have audible consequences when
the concept needs them. A music bed alone may leave all actions emotionally flat.
Listen to speech and music separately and in the final mix, including joins,
loop seams, fades and the ending. Meter values help diagnose problems but do not
establish musical character, intelligibility or a universal loudness target.

After changing the ending's length, check the music endpoint: a shorter film may
cut off its fade. Vids may transcribe voice and music separately; music can add
unwanted caption words, while a voice track can acquire an extra final word.
See [caption correction](russian-and-speech.md#captions-and-time-domains) and verify
each affected layer.
